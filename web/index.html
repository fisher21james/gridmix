See what's powering your grid, right now.

A 4.3" animated desk display. Turbines spin with the wind, the coal plant smokes when coal is burning, and the header shows what your power costs this minute.

What it shows

Everything updates on its own once it's set up.

A living scene

Each power source is a little building that comes alive in proportion to how much it's producing: spinning turbines, smoking stacks, a pouring dam, glinting solar panels.

The mix at a glance

A big renewable percentage, plus a colour-matched bar of every source. Tap the bar to switch between a labelled and a slim style.

Your price, in plain English

Enter your own tariff and it shows your rate right now and which time-of-use period you're in. A simple grid status (clean & cheap, normal, busy or under strain) tells you when it's a good time to run big appliances.

Your area or your whole country

Tap the region name to switch between your local grid and your whole country. Australia for now, with more countries coming in updates. Each view shows how fresh its data is.

Install

Straight from this page. No software to download.

Beta: the current firmware is only a hardware test (a spinning turbine and a touch check). It is not the finished display. The full firmware will install from this same button when it's ready.
This browser can't talk to USB devices. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a Windows, Mac or Linux computer. Installing needs a secure page. Open this page over https. Firmware v__VERSION__
  1. Plug the display into your computer with the USB-C data cable.
  2. Click Install Grid Mix and pick the board from the list. It usually shows as USB JTAG/serial debug unit.
  3. Wait about a minute. Don't unplug it.
  4. When asked, choose your Wi-Fi and enter the password. The display remembers it.

If it doesn't show up

  • Try another cable or USB port. Charge-only cables are the most common problem.
  • Hold the BOOT button on the back of the board while plugging it in, then click Install again.
  • Close anything else using the board, like the Arduino IDE.
  • Updating later? Click Install again. Your settings are kept unless you choose to erase.

Set up

Once it's on your Wi-Fi, open gridmix.local on your phone (same Wi-Fi) to choose your region and enter your tariff. Supported now: Australia. More countries are coming in updates. Try a demo of the setup page, or try the screen in your browser.

Get your free data key

A one-off job that takes about two minutes. The key is like a library card: it lets your display ask for grid data. Open the guide for where you live.

Australia: Open Electricity
  1. Go to platform.openelectricity.org.au and create a free account.
  2. Click API Keys in the menu on the left, then the button to create a new key.
  3. Name the key Gridmix, set Expiry to Never, and click Create Key.
  4. Copy the key straight away with the copy button. It starts with oe_ and is only shown once. Keep it somewhere safe, then click I've saved my key.
  5. Paste the key into your display's setup page (gridmix.local) when it asks.

Keep your key private, like a password. If it ever ends up somewhere public, delete it on the API Keys page and make a new one.

Outside Australia? More countries are planned for future updates, once there's a free source with up-to-date data for them.

Questions

Does my data go anywhere?

No. The display talks directly to the data source you pick. There's no account and no cloud service in between, and your tariff stays on the device.

Why do I need my own key?

The data sources are free but limit how often each key can ask. Your own key means your display is never slowed down by anyone else's.

Can I use the resistive or no-touch version of the board?

Not yet. The firmware is built for the capacitive version. Support for the others may come later.

Will it work in my country?

Right now it supports Australia. More countries will be added in future updates as free, up-to-date data sources are confirmed (the US is first on the list), and you'll get them by clicking Install again.

Why not Safari or Firefox?

Installing uses Web Serial, which only Chrome and Edge support on desktop. You only need it once; after that the display updates itself.

Is it open source?

Yes. The firmware and this page are on GitHub under the MIT licence.
